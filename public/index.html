What we found

Findings

  • The fine-tune is real. N-ATLaS beats its Llama 3.1 base by chrF++ on average. English to Yoruba goes from 7.6 to 24.5.
  • It writes Hausa close to the best. English to Hausa scores 47.4, against 51.7 for the top hosted model.
  • It is weakest translating into English. Hausa to English scores 51.2 against 67.2; Yoruba to English 37.6 against 57.1.
  • Yoruba is hard for every model. No model passes 33 for English to Yoruba, and ignoring tone marks lifts every model by about 10 points.
  • The cheapest hosted model still wins on quality. Claude Haiku 5.5 beat N-ATLaS in all six directions; its 600 translations cost $0.03.
  • N-ATLaS's advantage is where it runs. It runs offline on a laptop or a server in Nigeria, with no text or audio leaving the machine.
  • One correction to the model card. N-ATLaS is built on Llama 3.1 8B (its config uses Llama 3.1's rope scaling and 128k positions), not Llama 3.
Method

How we tested, and the limits

  • Data. Google's FLEURS test set: read speech of FLORES sentences, which are also aligned across languages for translation. CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • Speech. NCAIR1 Hausa, Igbo and Yoruba ASR models (fine-tuned Whisper Small) against stock openai/whisper-small, greedy decoding.
  • Translation. One fixed prompt for every model, temperature 0, first line of output kept. N-ATLaS and Llama 3.1 ran locally in 4-bit on an Apple M5 with MLX; hosted models ran through OpenRouter.
  • Sample size. 100 items per language and direction. This is a first pass, not a leaderboard.
  • Quantization. The 4-bit local models may score a point or two below full precision.
  • Contamination. FLEURS and FLORES are public, so any model here may have seen them in training.
  • Real speech. FLEURS is clean read speech with formal vocabulary. Street voice notes will score differently.
